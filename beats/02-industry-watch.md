BEAT: what actually changed this week in <YOUR INDUSTRY> — the terms, the prices, the
rules — with weight on <YOUR REGION> and on <SPECIFIC COMPANIES YOU FOLLOW>.

(Replace the placeholders. This example is written for a consumer-terms beat — loyalty
programmes, insurance, telco plans, banking fees — where the story is usually a change
buried in a document nobody reads. Adapt the sources to your field.)

WHERE TO LOOK. Sweep the last 24-72 hours:
  - The organisation's OWN pages: terms and conditions, price lists, rate cards,
    changelogs, release notes.
  - Investor material — earnings calls and filings say more about where something is
    heading than the marketing does.
  - The trade press and enthusiast communities — for LEADS ONLY, never as the source
    of a number.

WHAT MAKES A GOOD ONE. A change and what it actually costs a real customer. A price
rise dressed as an upgrade. An "enhancement" that is a cut. A quiet clause with large
consequences. The best of these show the arithmetic the announcement leaves out.

⚠️ VERIFICATION. This is where numbers rot fastest and secondary coverage gets them
wrong most often. Every rate, fee, price and date must come from the ORGANISATION'S OWN
page or announcement, and you must state the date you retrieved it, because these pages
change without notice. If a blog reports a change, confirm it against the primary
document before repeating a single figure. Changes are frequently announced with a
future effective date — never state a future change as already in force.

⚠️ DISCLOSURE — THE MOST IMPORTANT RULE IN THIS FILE.
If you have ANY connection to an organisation you cover — you work there, you hold
equity, you consult for them, you run a competing product, you were paid — the video
must say so, in the narration AND in the description, plainly and without softening it.

If you have no connection but the video could be mistaken for official communication,
say that too: "independent analysis from public sources, not affiliated with, endorsed
by, or produced with the cooperation of <ORGANISATION>."

Never present any of this as financial or purchasing advice.
