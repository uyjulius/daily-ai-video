BEAT: what happened in the market and what the competing explanations for it are.
Set your own indices and region here — e.g. a home market plus one global one.

WHERE TO LOOK. The most recent COMPLETED trading sessions:
  - Index levels and moves, from the exchange or a primary data source.
  - Regulatory filings and earnings releases for anything that moved on news — the
    filing itself, not the summary of it.
  - Central bank communications, rate expectations, and the macro prints that actually
    landed this week.

WHAT MAKES A GOOD ONE. Not a recap — nobody needs another one. The interesting thing is
almost always that the same move has two or three incompatible explanations being
offered at once, and that the data can adjudicate between some of them. Concentration in
a handful of names, a divergence between price and earnings, a local story the
globally-focused narrative misses — those are the shapes worth 20 minutes.

⚠️ VERIFICATION — this beat is the easiest one to get badly wrong.
  - EVERY figure carries an explicit as-of date and time, in the narration and in the
    description. A market number without a timestamp is worthless and misleading.
  - Take levels and moves from the exchange, not from a headline. Headlines round, and
    they recycle yesterday's number as today's.
  - Distinguish the completed session from live/after-hours movement. Say which.
  - Never state a projection, target or forecast as fact. Attribute every forecast to
    whoever made it, with its date.

⚠️ LIST YOUR SOURCES IN THE DESCRIPTION. Audited 22 Aug 2026: every non-markets video
listed 6-12 sources in its description; the three markets videos listed 0, 1 and 1. This
is the beat where sourcing matters most — market figures rot fastest and are the easiest
to get subtly wrong — and it was the only beat publishing nothing checkable. The
description must carry a PRIMARY SOURCES block naming, for every figure used: the
exchange or filing it came from, and the date and time it was read. A reader must be
able to re-derive your numbers without watching the video.

⚠️ THIS IS NOT ADVICE, AND THE VIDEO MUST SAY SO. No price targets, no buy/sell/hold
framing, no "this is undervalued", no portfolio suggestions. Explain what happened and
what the competing readings are; let the viewer draw conclusions. The description must
carry an explicit "not financial advice, for information only" statement and the
research timestamp. If a topic cannot be covered without implying a recommendation,
change the topic.

⚠️ Consider whether you should run this beat at all. Financial content carries
regulatory expectations that vary by country, and an unattended daily pipeline is a
poor place to discover yours. Check before enabling it.
