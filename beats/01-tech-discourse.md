BEAT: what the conversation in your field is actually saying this week — with the
emphasis on how practitioners really work, as opposed to how vendors say they do.

WHERE TO LOOK. This beat is sourced from the conversation itself, not from press
releases. Sweep the last 24-72 hours across:
  - Hacker News (news.ycombinator.com) — front page AND high-comment threads. The
    comments are usually the story; look for practitioners contradicting the article.
  - Reddit — the subreddits where people in your field actually work, not the ones
    where they cheer. Weight heavily-discussed threads over highly-upvoted ones.
  - X / Mastodon / Bluesky — people posting directly. Their own posts, not
    screenshots of them.
Also read the thing being discussed — the paper, repo, blog post or filing underneath.

WHAT MAKES A GOOD ONE. A gap between claim and practice. Practitioners describing what
actually happens when they deploy the thing. A public statement colliding with what
that organisation's own staff report. A benchmark result the people in the thread do
not believe, and why. Rough consensus forming, or breaking.
Avoid: launch hype, funding rounds, and threads that are only sentiment with nothing
checkable underneath.

⚠️ THE VERIFICATION RULE FOR THIS BEAT — it differs from the others, read it carefully.
Two DIFFERENT kinds of claim appear here and they need different evidence:
  1. "PERSON X SAID Y." The primary source is the post, thread or transcript itself.
     Link it. Quote exactly. If it is a screenshot, find the original — screenshots are
     routinely faked or stripped of context. If you cannot find the original, cut it.
  2. "Y IS TRUE." A thousand upvotes is not evidence. The primary source is whatever
     the claim is about — the paper, the docs, the filing, the code. Verify separately.
NEVER let the popularity of a claim stand in for its truth. The failure mode of this
beat is laundering a confident forum comment into a stated fact, and it is the single
thing most likely to embarrass a channel.

Anonymous accounts are anonymous. Say "an engineer claiming to work at X" — never state
an unverified person's employer as fact. When you name a real, identifiable person,
quote them accurately, link the source, and represent their view as they stated it.
